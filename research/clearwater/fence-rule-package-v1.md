# Clearwater Fence Rule Package v1

## 1. Source Authority and Currency

This is a research and rule-structuring package, not legal advice and not production
logic. Its primary authority is the substantive text of the City of Clearwater
Community Development Code (CDC), principally Article 3, Division 8, §§ 3-801
through 3-808. The source extraction identifies itself as Volume II, Supplement
No. 55 (July 2026), containing ordinances through Ordinance No. 9857-25, enacted
December 4, 2025. Individual pages within Division 8 carry earlier supplement
numbers; this package treats them as pages retained in the current compiled volume,
but does not independently establish whether an uncodified ordinance postdating the
stated cutoff changes a rule.

The operative Division 8 text, rather than its table of contents, was used. The
extraction is two-column text and sometimes interleaves the left and right columns.
The section headings, paragraphs, ordinance histories, and page continuations make
§§ 3-801 through 3-808 recoverable. The two sight-triangle figures in § 3-904 and
the illustrative fence figures in § 3-804 are not textually recoverable as geometry;
no dimensions have been inferred from them.

Secondary sources are the prior Clearwater fence research files. Code of Ordinances
rules are retained as supplemental special cases, not allowed to displace the CDC's
ordinary-fence rules. Where those extracts were incomplete or of mixed vintage, that
limitation remains.

**Authority hierarchy used here**

1. CDC §§ 3-801–3-808 for ordinary fences and walls.
2. Express CDC cross-references, including §§ 3-901, 3-903, 3-904, 3-1202 and
   Article 8 definitions.
3. Supported Code of Ordinances special cases from the prior master.
4. External codes, plans, engineering documents, parcel instruments, and official
   decisions only as dependencies; no absent content is inferred.

## 2. Applicability

### Section-by-section reconstruction

#### § 3-801 — Purpose and applicability

- **Applicability and rule:** Division 8 provides standards for fences and walls.
- **Numbers, exceptions, and discretion:** None stated.
- **Implementation:** Establishes scope but is not an affirmative statement that
  every fence is permitted.
- **Deterministic:** Yes as a scope statement; no as a complete permission test.

#### § 3-802 — Materials

- **Applicability:** All fences and walls under Division 8.
- **Operative requirements:** Fence materials must be appropriate to purpose and
  location; walls must have a decorative finish such as masonry, brick, stucco, or
  paint; corrugated or sheet metal may not form a fence or wall; electric or other
  potentially injurious, hazardous, or noxious materials or construction techniques
  are prohibited; wire fences must use chain-link wire; chain-link fences may not
  have exposed points, wires, or prongs at the top.
- **Barbed wire exception:** Barbed wire is permitted only atop a chain-link fence
  at least **six feet** high, oriented toward the property's interior, when necessary
  to preclude entry into a hazardous location or facility or to protect exterior
  storage of materials or equipment from vandalism or theft, and only if the property
  is not adjacent to residentially zoned or residentially used property.
- **Discretion/definitions:** “Appropriate,” “potentially injurious,” “hazardous,”
  “noxious,” “necessary,” “adjacent,” and “decorative finish” need judgment or
  definitions not supplied in Division 8. The listed wall finishes are examples,
  not an exclusive list.
- **Deterministic:** Partial.

#### § 3-803 — Design requirements

- **Applicability:** Fences and walls; district design standards control first in
  Downtown (D), US 19, and Tourist (T) Districts and the Coachman Ridge (CRNCOD) and
  Island Estates (IENCOD) neighborhood conservation overlays. Division 8 fills
  subjects on which those standards or guidelines are silent.
- **Finished side:** In a required setback, the side facing or viewable from a
  street right-of-way or adjoining property must be the finished side, with support
  posts and stringers facing inward.
- **Blocked-access exception:** If a building, fence, wall, other structure, or dense
  foliage on adjoining property wholly or partly blocks access to a fence portion,
  support posts (but not stringers) for that portion may face outward. The adjoining
  obstruction need not itself adjoin the property line.
- **Long street frontage:** A fence or wall exceeding **100 feet** in one horizontal
  plane along a street right-of-way must either (a) be offset to make landscaped
  inset areas at least **eight feet wide and eight feet deep**, or (b) provide
  non-opaque openings using wrought iron or similar wall treatment/architectural
  features such as a column to break up the unbroken plane.
- **Maintenance access:** A fence or wall in a required setback must have an opening
  or gate wide enough to allow access outside the enclosure to maintain required
  landscaping and street right-of-way, unless the Community Development Coordinator
  determines this impracticable.
- **Visibility:** Every fence and wall must comply with § 3-904.A.
- **Deterministic:** Partial; “finished,” “dense foliage,” “similar,” “sufficient
  width,” and impracticability require facts or judgment.

#### § 3-804 — Height requirements

- **Applicability:** All fences and walls **except chain-link fences**. Chain-link is
  governed by § 3-805.
- **Front baseline:** Between the principal structure and front property line,
  maximum **four feet**. A fence or wall over **three feet** high must have a
  **three-foot-wide** landscaped strip on its right-of-way side, consistent with
  § 3-1202, unless the Community Development Coordinator determines otherwise.
- **Front exceptions/approvals:**
  - In LDR, LMDR, MDR, and MHDR, a brick or other masonry wall with columns linked
    by substantial grillwork may reach **six feet** in a required front setback only
    through Level One flexible-standard approval and must be architecturally
    compatible with the principal structure and surrounding properties.
  - In HDR, MHP, C, T, O, I, IRT, OSR, and P, an open-style fence or wall with
    separated pickets, or masonry columns linked by substantial grillwork, may reach
    **six feet** in front of a principal structure. Any other style may be requested
    through Level One flexible-standard approval.
  - In the T District area governed by Beach by Design, residential uses may request
    up to **six feet**; all other uses may request up to **four feet** through Level
    One approval, provided the fence/wall is primarily open style (separated pickets
    or masonry columns with substantial grillwork), meets Beach by Design, and is
    integrated into required landscaping.
  - A residential-subdivision perimeter wall in any zoning district may reach
    **six feet** and must be architecturally compatible with subdivision building
    design.
  - Corner and multi-frontage lots may have a fence up to **six feet** under
    § 3-804.C.
- **Side/rear baseline:** Between the principal structure and a side or rear property
  line, maximum **six feet**.
- **Side/rear exceptions:**
  - Up to **eight feet** in IRT.
  - Up to **eight feet** in C through Level One approval to buffer drive-through
    facilities, vehicle sales/displays, automobile service stations, outdoor retail
    sales/display/storage, and residential zoning districts. If part of Level Two
    review, the Community Development Board decides.
  - On property adjacent to water, a fence within **20 feet** of the property line
    adjacent to water or within the required setback, **whichever is greater**, must
    be non-opaque and no more than **four feet** high.
- **Corner/multi-frontage lots:** The addressed front property line is the front for
  fence placement; other front property lines are treated as side lines. The actual
  front follows § 3-804.A. A side-line fence may follow § 3-804.B only if it is
  consistent with adjoining-property character, structure placement, and setbacks,
  including property-line placement, as determined by the Coordinator; and it is
  primarily open style with separated pickets. If that side is adjacent to an
  arterial or collector right-of-way as classified by the City Engineer, the fence
  may follow § 3-804.B regardless of neighboring placement and may be solid/opaque.
  Any such fence over **three feet** requires the same **three-foot-wide** right-of-
  way-side landscape strip unless the Coordinator determines otherwise.
- **Double-frontage lots:** The addressed front is the front; the opposite front line
  is treated as rear. The addressed front follows § 3-804.A. A fence on the rear
  property line may follow § 3-804.B only where the rear of both adjacent lots on the
  same side of the street and the dwellings across the street are likewise oriented
  with their rears toward that street. If both conditions are not met, the rear fence
  may not exceed **four feet**. If the rear abuts an arterial or collector
  right-of-way as classified by the City Engineer, § 3-804.B applies regardless of
  those conditions.
- **Attached dwellings:** No fence/wall over **six feet** on an attached-dwelling
  lot, except a uniformly designed boundary fence/wall that meets otherwise
  applicable height limits and standards.
- **Retaining walls:** A retaining wall (other than one associated with a detention
  pond regulated by § 3-901) may lie between the principal structure and any lot line
  if no above-grade portion exceeds **18 inches**, unless the City Engineer determines
  a greater height is required for an environmental or engineering need.
- **Combinations:** Fence, wall, landscape berm, and retaining wall may be combined,
  but their combined height may not exceed the applicable maximum.
- **Entry components:** A gate, arbor, trellis, pergola, or similar access component
  incorporated into an opening may be no more than **eight feet overall**, extend no
  more than **one foot horizontally** on each side of the opening, and project no more
  than **three feet from the face** of the fence/wall.
- **Deterministic:** Partial. Baselines and measurements are objective once parcel
  geometry and classification are known; approvals, compatibility, street
  classification, neighboring pattern, and engineering determinations are not.

#### § 3-805 — Chainlink fences

- **Applicability:** Chain-link fences; spelling follows the source concept although
  its heading uses “Chainlink.”
- **Downtown:** Prohibited in the Downtown District.
- **Front:** Not permitted in front of a principal structure; permitted only to the
  rear of the principal structure's front building line.
- **Side/rear:** Between principal structure and side/rear lot line, maximum
  **48 inches**, or **six feet** if clad with green or black vinyl. If the side/rear
  lot line is adjacent to a public right-of-way, chain-link is prohibited there.
- **Landscaping:** A continuous hedge or nondeciduous robust-growing vine at frequent
  intervals must landscape the full fence length, on either the external or internal
  side.
- **Recreation exception:** Chain-link for public/private tennis courts, golf courses
  and driving ranges, athletic fields, play courts, batting cages, and similar uses
  is exempt from § 3-805's height rules and § 3-805.B's front-location restriction.
- **Vacant lots:** Except Downtown, green- or black-vinyl-clad chain-link may secure a
  vacant lot up to **six feet**, subject to § 3-805.D landscaping; it must be removed
  when the lot is developed.
- **Public landbank:** A **six-foot** green- or black-vinyl-coated chain-link fence may
  surround publicly owned landbanked property and is exempt from § 3-805.D
  landscaping.
- **Deterministic:** Mostly, but “similar uses,” “continuous,” “robust growing,”
  “frequent intervals,” “vacant,” and “developed” may need interpretation.

#### § 3-806 — Easements and rights-of-way

- **Baseline:** No fence/wall may be in any easement or right-of-way or enclose a
  water meter box or manhole, subject to the listed exceptions.
- **Public/drainage land:** A fence/wall may not be in a public right-of-way,
  right-of-way easement, or drainage easement unless specifically authorized by the
  City Engineer.
- **Meters/manholes:** May not be enclosed unless specifically authorized by the
  City Engineer.
- **Utility easements:** A fence/wall may be permitted if it provides a suitable gate
  or opening allowing access to utilities, meters, or similar facilities, and the
  city or franchised utility may remove it without cost or obligation to replace or
  restore fence, wall, or landscaping as necessary to maintain utilities.
- **Public projects:** Deviations may be permitted for fences on publicly owned land
  associated with public projects through Level One flexible-standard approval.
- **Deterministic:** Partial. Recorded spatial facts are objective; “suitable” access,
  specific authorization, maintenance necessity, and Level One approval are not.

#### § 3-807 — Special regulations

- **Swimming pools:** A swimming pool may be enclosed with a **four-foot-high** fence
  or wall. “May” does not establish whether another authority requires a barrier or
  prescribes openings, gates, latches, alarms, or other safety details.
- **Visibility:** All fences/walls must comply with Article 3, Division 9 sight-
  visibility-triangle requirements.
- **Subdivision plans:** When subdivision approval required a fence/wall, additional
  or substitute fences/walls that contravene the approved plan's general purpose and
  uniformity are prohibited.
- **Subdivision perimeter:** A **six-foot** wall or non-chain-link fence may surround
  a residential subdivision if it does not conflict with § 3-807.C.1 or § 3-804.A.
- **Construction sites:** Temporary construction-site fences may be approved by the
  Building Official and must comply with reasonable conditions the official sets,
  including possible height, location, and materials conditions.
- **Deterministic:** Partial. The two stated heights are objective, but an approved
  subdivision plan, “contravene,” “uniformity,” Building Official approval, and
  reasonable project-specific conditions prevent fully automatic disposition.

#### § 3-808 — Maintenance of fences and walls

- **Applicability:** Every fence/wall constructed under Division 8.
- **General standard:** Maintain it structurally sound and aesthetically attractive.
- **Specific standards:** It must remain vertical and not sag/lean more than
  **10 degrees from vertical**, unless designed and permitted for that angle; rotten
  boards must be replaced; posts/footers must be solidly attached to ground;
  stringers must be securely attached to posts and the fence face; the fence must be
  securely attached to posts and stringers; and surfaces must be painted, stained,
  treated, or otherwise maintained for a uniform appearance. New replacement
  material may take time to age/weather to match original material.
- **Nonconforming fences:** Boards may be replaced if posts are not replaced.
- **Deterministic:** Partial. The 10-degree limit and listed physical conditions are
  testable; “structurally sound,” “aesthetically attractive,” “securely,” and
  “uniform appearance” require inspection/judgment.

## 3. General Materials Rules

The controlling general rules are § 3-802's material provisions reconstructed above.
They apply alongside, not instead of, § 3-805's chain-link regime and Code of
Ordinances § 28.06's narrower street/alley/sidewalk barbed-wire prohibition.

No source reviewed establishes a general rule for wood species, vinyl paneling,
razor wire, fence-post depth, mesh size, or masonry engineering. Their absence from
this package is not permission.

## 4. Design Rules

Apply § 3-803 in this order:

1. Identify D, US 19, T, CRNCOD, or IENCOD status and apply the applicable design
   standards/guidelines first.
2. Use Division 8 where those standards/guidelines are silent.
3. If the fence/wall is in a required setback, apply finished-side orientation and
   maintenance-access rules.
4. If it exceeds 100 feet in a single plane along a street right-of-way, apply the
   offset/opening/architectural-treatment rule.
5. Apply § 3-904.A visibility in every case.

The current extraction contains CDC district and overlay provisions, but the
incorporated Beach by Design guidelines and reliable mapped applicability are not
fully resolved in this package. A parcel in one of the five named regimes cannot be
answered solely with the Division 8 baseline.

## 5. Height Rules

### Decision hierarchy

1. Determine whether the proposal is chain-link; if yes, use § 3-805 rather than
   § 3-804's ordinary height schedule.
2. Determine relation to principal structure and property lines.
3. Determine corner, multi-frontage, double-frontage, attached-dwelling, waterfront,
   residential-subdivision-perimeter, retaining-wall, and entry-component status.
4. Determine zoning and applicable D/US 19/T/CRNCOD/IENCOD standards.
5. Apply visibility restrictions independently; a nominal height allowance does not
   authorize obstruction in a sight triangle.
6. Treat Level One/Two and official determinations as approval pathways, not automatic
   entitlements.

highest point under § 8-102's definition of “Height, building or structure.” Flood-
elevation clauses in that definition specifically discuss buildings; this package
does not extend them to fences without authority.

## 6. Front / Side / Rear / Street-Side Rules

- “Front” for the ordinary rule is the area between principal structure and front
  property line.
- “Side/rear” is the area between principal structure and the corresponding line.
- A corner or multi-frontage parcel uses its addressed frontage as front and treats
  other front property lines as sides **for fence placement**, subject to § 3-804.C.
- A double-frontage parcel uses the addressed frontage as front and treats the
  opposite frontage as rear **for fence placement**, subject to § 3-804.D.
- A chain-link fence has its own front-building-line and public-right-of-way rules.
- “Street-side” is not a standalone defined yard in the reviewed text. It must be
  derived through the corner/multi-frontage rules; Groundrule must not invent a
  separate street-side maximum.
- Section 3-903.A expressly excepts fences and walls from the general prohibition on
  structures in required setbacks. That exception allows Division 8 to regulate
  fences there; it does not erase Division 8, visibility, easement, overlay, or
  approval restrictions.
- Section 3-903.D says corner lots have two front and two side setbacks. For fence
  placement, § 3-804.C's addressed-front method controls the special classification.
- Section 3-1202.F resolves what the referenced front landscape strip contains: it
  must be outside the right-of-way and consist of a solid hedge or a mix such as
  shrubs, small trees, flowers, and vines, installed at species-appropriate intervals
  along the entire fence/wall sufficient to provide coverage at maturity.

## 7. Chainlink Fence Rules

Section 3-805 is a separate regime, not merely a material option within § 3-804.
Before answering, collect: Downtown status, principal-structure/front-building-line
geometry, side/rear line and public-right-of-way adjacency, vinyl color/cladding,
landscaping, vacant/developed status, public landbank ownership, and recreational-
facility use. Section 3-802.G's prohibition on exposed top points/wires/prongs also
applies. Barbed wire creates an apparent interaction: § 3-802.E conditionally permits
it atop six-foot chain-link while § 3-802.G bars exposed points/wires/prongs on top.
The text does not explain that interaction. **Human interpretation is required before
approving any barbed-wire design.** Code of Ordinances § 28.06 independently
prohibits building or repairing barbed-wire fencing adjacent to a street, alley, or
sidewalk.

## 8. Easements and Rights-of-Way

Use surveyed/recorded parcel data rather than user estimates. Section 3-806 supplies
the fence-specific rule previously missing from the Code of Ordinances research.
It does not quantify gate width or establish an automatic authorization process.
The recorded easement instrument may impose additional private or utility terms;
those are parcel-specific external authority.

## 9. Visibility / Sight-Triangle Rules

Section 3-904.A prohibits a structure or landscaping that obstructs views between
**30 inches above grade and eight feet above grade** within the street/driveway sight
visibility triangle shown in its figure, unless the City Engineer approves otherwise.
A fence is expressly directed to comply by §§ 3-803.F and 3-807.B.

Section 3-904.B separately protects water views: within the waterfront sight triangle
shown in its figure, no structure or landscaping may be installed except an at-grade
pool, at-grade deck, or **non-opaque fence no higher than 48 inches**.

The source extraction does not preserve the figures' horizontal distances or method
of locating triangle vertices. Consequently:

- the vertical obstruction band and waterfront fence exception are supported;
- whether a proposed segment lies inside either triangle is not determinable from
  the text alone;
- no horizontal dimension may be inferred; and
- the official figures or authoritative GIS geometry are **EXTERNAL AUTHORITY
  REQUIRED** before automated spatial evaluation.

## 10. Swimming Pool and Other Special Fence Rules

### Pools

Section 3-807.A says a pool may be enclosed with a four-foot-high fence or wall. It
does not say a barrier is optional under all law and does not provide opening,
climbability, gate, latch, alarm, separation, permit, or inspection standards. The
current Florida Building Code/local technical code and official permitting material
are **EXTERNAL AUTHORITY REQUIRED** for a complete pool-barrier answer.

Section 3-903.H concerns setbacks for swimming pools and screened pool enclosures,
not ordinary fence setbacks. It requires the principal-use setbacks of the applicable
zoning district unless district flexibility permits otherwise.

### Other Division 8 special cases

- Approved subdivision fence/wall schemes and subdivision perimeter enclosures:
  § 3-807.C.
- Temporary construction-site fencing: § 3-807.D, entirely approval/condition based.
- Retaining walls: § 3-804.F.
- Gates/arbors/trellises/pergolas/similar entry components: § 3-804.H.
- Recreational chain-link, vacant lots, and public landbanks: § 3-805.E–G.

## 11. Maintenance Rules

Apply § 3-808's general and specific duties throughout the fence's life. Board-only
replacement on a nonconforming fence is expressly allowed only when posts are not
replaced. This package does not infer that replacing posts is always prohibited; it
may instead trigger current-code/nonconformity review, for which Article 6 and permit
requirements would need separate analysis.

## 12. Supplemental Code of Ordinances Rules

These supported prior findings survive reconciliation and must remain separately
tagged from ordinary Division 8 logic:

1. **§ 28.06 — barbed wire near streets.** Building or repairing a barbed-wire fence
   within the city adjacent to a street, alley, or sidewalk is unlawful. “Adjacent”
   is unquantified. This narrow prohibition applies in addition to § 3-802.E.
2. **§ 32.336 — Clearwater Gas System access.** A designated gas meter/service
   regulator or propane LP tank location must remain readily accessible. The source
   says the designated point is typically within ten feet of the premises corner
   nearest the gas main and is not expected to be enclosed by fencing or hedges.
   Ten feet is descriptive, **not** a fence setback.
3. **§ 32.284 — containerized-service waste enclosures.** Retain only for applicable
   solid-waste enclosures: visibility screening, truck access, **12 feet** of
   unobstructed access, and a **six-inch-thick** reinforced-concrete pad are supported,
   but covered classes, subsection (5) relief, CDC § 3-201.D, Engineering Index 701,
   and director specifications remain unresolved. Do not generalize it to residential
   boundary fences.
4. **§ 11.04(1) — cemetery enclosures.** Aboveground enclosures are prohibited in
   cemetery sections 14, 15, and 16. This is not ordinary parcel logic.
5. **Likely § 28.95 — utility-construction silt fences.** The extract requires silt
   fences for governed utility work adjacent to surface-water bodies or wetlands,
   but the parent citation/scope is not fully verified. This is erosion control.
6. **Charter § 2.01 waterfront security clause.** A narrow city-owned waterfront
   property's public-access clause does not prohibit security fencing/gates. It is
   not a private-parcel entitlement and needs the complete charter subsection and
   implementing resolutions.
7. **§ 28.08 right-of-way objects.** No longer needed to infer ordinary-fence
   applicability because § 3-806 directly governs fences. Retain only as possible
   additional encroachment/enforcement authority pending full review.

Airport cross-references, business-tax category 038.170, repealed barriers,
ratproofing, assessment, address-number, driveway, and affordable-housing hits remain
non-rules or unresolved dependencies exactly as explained in the prior master.

## 13. Definitions Required for Evaluation

### Definitions resolved in CDC § 8-102

- **Community Development Coordinator:** planning-department director or designee.
- **Development approval:** city approval, permit, or other official action granting,
  denying, or conditionally granting an application for development.
- **Easement:** grant of property right(s) by an owner to/for public use or another
  person/entity.
- **Height, building or structure:** for other structures, vertical distance from
  existing grade to the structure's highest point above that grade.
- **Lot:** land used, developed, built upon, or proposed as one unit under single
  ownership.
- **Corner lot:** lot at intersection of two or more street rights-of-way.
- **Double-frontage lot:** lot bounded on opposite or approximately opposite sides by
  streets.
- **Multi-frontage lot:** lot bounded by three or more street rights-of-way.
- **Opaque:** nontranslucent/nontransparent material providing a visual barrier from
  one side to the other.
- **Principal structure/use:** main or primary structure/use of land.
- **Front property line:** line generally parallel to and along a road right-of-way
  or street, excluding alleys; side line runs generally perpendicular or radial to
  it.
- **Right-of-way:** land reserved, used, or intended for vehicular/pedestrian travel,
  including highways, streets, alleys, walkways, drainage facilities, or other public
  purpose, public or private.
- **Setback/front/rear/side setback:** required horizontal distance between the
  corresponding property line and a structure.

### Definitions/interpretations still required

No reviewed operative definition adequately resolves: fence, wall, chain-link,
finished side, front building line, existing grade as applied along a fence,
open-style/primarily open, substantial grillwork, street-side, adjacent, residentially
used, hazardous location/facility, vacant lot, developed lot, landbanked property,
continuous hedge, frequent intervals, robust-growing vine, suitable access opening,
swimming-pool fence versus required pool barrier, or how combined berm/wall/fence
height is measured. Do not invent meanings.

## 20. Readiness Assessment

### READY WITH LIMITED OPEN DEPENDENCIES

The source package is sufficient to begin **designing** a Supabase rule schema for an
ordinary residential fence workflow. It now supports auditable entities for rule
authority, fence type/material, segment location, height, lot/frontage type,
waterfront condition, zoning/overlay selector, exceptions, approval requirements,
spatial dependencies, qualitative review, and confidence. It is not ready for a
fully automated final “can I build it?” decision or production population.

Exact blockers to complete automation are:

1. official horizontal sight-triangle geometry;
2. pool-barrier technical authority;
3. ordinary permit/approval requirements;
4. authoritative parcel zoning/overlay/frontage/easement and street-classification
   inputs;
5. special district/overlay/Beach by Design standards for affected parcels;
6. unresolved qualitative/administrative determinations; and
7. the barbed-wire interaction identified in §§ 7 and 19.

Schema design should preserve `deterministic = partial/no`, external-authority and
official-approval states, rather than coercing any of these blockers into false or
permission-by-absence.
