## 2. Terminology and authoritative provisions

* **Swimming pool:** § 8-102 defines an in-ground or above-ground tank or tub for
  swimming or wading, including hot tubs, spas, whirlpools, jacuzzis, and similar
  installations.
* **Setback:** § 8-102 defines the required horizontal distance between a property
  line and a structure; directional setbacks use the corresponding property line.
* **Pool and associated deck classification:** § 3-204.H states that swimming pools
  and associated decks **12 inches or less above grade** are accessory structures;
  those **greater than 12 inches above grade** are principal structures.
* **Accessory-structure placement:** §§ 3-203.A–B require accessory structures to
  be on the same parcel as the principal use and to comply with applicable zoning-
  district setbacks. Section 3-903.A generally prohibits a building or structure
  within a required setback.
* **Pool/enclosure incorporation rule:** § 3-903.H requires swimming pools and
  screened pool enclosures to comply with the setbacks required by the applicable
  zoning district for the principal use, unless district flexibility permits
  otherwise.
* **Coastal construction control line:** § 3-903.E applies that line's setback when
  it is more restrictive than the zoning-district setback.

## 3. Ordinary LMDR setback framework and canonical property

For a detached-dwelling principal use in LMDR, Table 2-202 supplies minimum
setbacks of **25 feet front, 5 feet side, and 10 feet rear**. Section 3-903.H
incorporates those principal-use setbacks for the swimming pool and screened pool
enclosure. Sections 3-203, 3-204.H, and 3-903.A apply the structure/setback
framework to the associated deck as well.

For the trusted canonical profile, **1950 DREW PLZ → Clearwater → LMDR**, Groundrule
could therefore deterministically select the ordinary 25/5/10 framework. It could
not determine whether a proposed plan complies, because it has no surveyed
property-line or proposed pool/deck/enclosure geometry.

This ordinary result is not a waterfront answer. Table 2-202 footnote 1 states a
different LMDR waterfront detached-dwelling rear treatment (generally 25 feet,
with adjacent-structure and Building Code/seawall conditions). A corner lot has
two front and two side setbacks under § 3-903.D. A qualifying recorded double-
frontage restriction can change nonfrontage treatment under § 3-903.C. The current
profile establishes neither condition.

## 4. Deck-measurement reconciliation

The current committed Code does not contain a standalone sentence saying “measure
to the edge of the pool deck.” It does, however, now expressly classify **the pool
and its associated deck** as a structure in § 3-204.H. Read with §§ 3-203.A–B,
3-903.A and 8-102, the associated deck may not extend into the required setback.
Accordingly, where the deck is the closest pool-project structure to a property
line, the setback distance terminates at the deck edge—not merely at the water or
pool shell.

This is materially consistent with Abi's warning and does not conflict with any
reviewed provision. Production provenance should remain precise:

* **Code-derived:** associated decks are structures; structures comply with the
  applicable setbacks; setbacks run from property line to structure.
* **Planner-validated operational interpretation:** residents should measure to the
  pool deck rather than the water, and failure to do so can stop work.

The concise resident wording recommended for a later implementation is:

> **Measure to the pool deck—not the water.** The pool and its associated deck must
> stay outside the required setback. A screened enclosure must also meet the same
> principal-use setbacks.

The qualifier about which element is closest is important: proposed geometry can
make the deck or screened enclosure the controlling edge. Groundrule should not
claim plan compliance without that geometry.

## 5. Waterfront, floodplain, and compensatory excavation

The Code treats these as separate concepts; they must not be collapsed:

* Table 2-202 footnote 1 and § 3-904.B use **waterfront** for zoning setback and
  water-view/sight-triangle provisions.
* Chapter 51 uses mapped **flood hazard area**, **special flood hazard area**,
  floodway, coastal high hazard area, and related technical terms.
* Section 51.301 requires an applicant proposing development wholly or partly in a
  flood hazard area to apply to the Floodplain Administrator (and Building Official
  when applicable) for required permits and approvals.
* Section 51.902 allows development/redevelopment/fill outside floodways when
  **compensatory excavation** is provided. Engineering studies and analyses must
  demonstrate hydraulic balance; excavation is bounded by seasonal high water and
  base flood elevation and may not adversely affect the special flood hazard area.
  The Floodplain Administrator may waive it if the applicant demonstrates no
  adverse effects.
* Section 51.401 requires flood-hazard-area plans to identify filling, grading,
  excavation, and proposed compensatory excavation.

Thus compensatory excavation is conditional, not universal, and Groundrule must
never calculate displaced volume, excavation volume, elevations, BFE compliance,
or waiver eligibility. A pool/deck/site project can involve grading, excavation,
paving, structures, or fill—all included within Chapter 51's definition of
development—but applicability depends on mapped and project facts plus technical
review.

Abi's statement that **any waterfront property should involve the Floodplain
Administrator** is useful operational guidance, but the reviewed Code does not say
that every waterfront parcel is in a flood hazard area. A future guide may show a
conditional “Waterfront property?” handoff attributed to planner practice, while
separately explaining the Code-mandated flood-hazard-area review. It must not infer
waterfront or flood status from the other.

## 6. Screen enclosure, barrier, easement, and permit treatment

### Screen enclosure

Section 3-903.H expressly applies the principal-use setbacks independently to a
screened pool enclosure. Section 3-203.E exempts a screen enclosure around a pool
from the ordinary accessory-structure height maximum, but limits it to the height
of the principal structure. Proposed geometry determines whether the enclosure is
the closest controlling element; V1 should explain this without deciding it.

### Barrier

Section 3-807.A says a pool may be enclosed by a four-foot-high fence or wall. That
sentence is not a complete pool-safety-barrier regime and does not establish gates,
latches, openings, alarms, inspections, or all Florida Building Code duties. A Pool
V1 should say only that pools have separate barrier requirements and route the
resident to verified official permitting material (or the existing Fence Guide),
without duplicating Fence rules.

### Easements and external rights

The reviewed general pool provisions do not grant construction rights in an
easement. A survey, plat, recorded instrument, utility requirements, and City or
rights-holder review can independently constrain placement. Future wording should
say that meeting zoning setbacks does not authorize interference with an easement,
utility, drainage, access, or other property right.

### Permit pathway — blocking gap

The Code's fee schedule includes swimming-pool permits and Chapter 51 separately
requires flood-hazard development approvals. Those facts support that a permit
path exists, but they do not verify the **current resident application/checklist
URL**, required plan/survey package, or current inspection pathway. Because official
web access failed and no committed pool checklist exists, the requested useful
official next-step action cannot be published confidently. This satisfies an
explicit stop condition, so production implementation is deferred.

## 9. Future CAD asset specification

Use a **3:2 landscape plan view**, with a mobile-safe central crop. Show parcel
boundary, house footprint, pool-deck outer edge, water/pool-shell edge, optional
screen-enclosure outline only if clearly distinguished, setback line, and one
dimension arrow from property boundary terminating at the closest applicable deck
edge. Label **“Measure here”** at the deck and **“Not here”** at the water.

Line hierarchy: parcel boundary heaviest; deck/enclosure controlling edges next;
pool/water and house medium; construction/reference lines light; dimension and
labels high-contrast. Do not place invented dimensions in the asset. Export a
responsive SVG with real text and grouped semantic elements. Minimum label size
must remain legible near 320 CSS pixels. Include the visible caption **“Example
only — not your property.”** Suggested alt text: “Plan-view example showing a pool
setback measured from the property boundary to the outer pool-deck edge, not to the
water edge.”
