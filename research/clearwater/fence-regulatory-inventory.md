### [§ 28.06 — Barbed wire near streets]

**Applicability:** Any person building or repairing a barbed-wire fence within the city when the fence is adjacent to a street, alley, or sidewalk. The excerpt does not define “adjacent.”

**Requirement:** It is unlawful to build or repair such a fence. This is a categorical location/material restriction; the excerpt states no height, setback, yard, zoning, opacity, gate, or permit alternative.

**Exceptions / conditions:** None stated.

**Source text:**
> “It shall be unlawful for any person to build or repair within the city any barbed wire fence adjacent to any street, alley or sidewalk.”

**Groundrule interpretation notes:** This is the only provision in the supplied excerpts that directly regulates an ordinary fence by material and location on a citywide basis. Do not infer that barbed wire is allowed elsewhere; other development, building, use, or safety provisions may separately control it.

**Confidence:** High

**Needs additional source review:** No

## Special Cases

### [Charter § 2.01 — Security fencing and gates at specified city-owned waterfront property]

**Applicability:** A narrow charter provision concerning city-owned property west of Osceola Avenue and east of Clearwater Harbor between Drew and Pierce Streets, including city-owned public docks, promenade, side-tie moorings, and up to 140 dedicated recreational, noncommercial boat slips. It does not govern an ordinary private parcel.

**Requirement:** Regulations adopted by city council must ensure public access to the docks, boardwalks, and promenade, but security fencing and gates are not prohibited.

**Exceptions / conditions:** The quoted clause is an express qualification to the public-access direction. It authorizes no dimensions, materials, locations, or gate specifications and should not be generalized beyond the described city property.

**Source text:**
> “City council shall adopt by resolution regulations that provide city residents first priority for the dedicated boat slips and which ensure public access to the docks, boardwalks and promenade, however nothing herein shall prohibit fencing and gates for security.”

**Groundrule interpretation notes:** Retained for completeness because it can affect whether barriers may exist at this particular waterfront civic site. It is not a private-property fence entitlement. The beginning of Charter § 2.01 and the exact subsection designation are absent from the excerpt.

**Confidence:** Medium

**Needs additional source review:** Yes — **NEEDS FULL SECTION REVIEW** of Charter § 2.01 and the city-council resolution(s) adopted under it.

### [§ 32.284 — Waste receptacle and recycling-container enclosures]

**Applicability:**

- Subsection (1) applies to customers receiving containerized service who are not within the (apparently erroneous or incomplete) exception described as “paragraph 5 subsection (b).”
- Subsection (2) adds rules for a containerized-service site whose use or improvement requires site-plan approval or approval of a minor change of use.
- Subsection (4)(a) applies to a residential customer serving a multiple-family residence who is not otherwise subject to § 32.321.

**Requirement:** A covered containerized-service customer must construct and maintain an enclosure so the waste receptacle is not visible from ground level. Its gate must be wide enough for access by a front-end-loading refuse truck, which requires **12 feet of unobstructed access**. The base must be a **six-inch-thick reinforced concrete pad** sized for the receptacle. Sites within subsection (2) must also comply with director-promulgated rules governing enclosure dimensions, materials, location, and other physical attributes. All enclosures must comply with Community Development Code § 3-201.D and City Engineering Index 701.

For the specified multiple-family residential customers, an enclosure for recycling containers must be located for easy resident and front-end-vehicle collector access and constructed so the containers are not visible from any public right-of-way. The solid waste/general services director or designee reviews its proposed location and construction for these criteria and applicable building and safety codes.

No final building inspection may occur and no certificate of occupancy may issue for a site that has not complied with § 32.284. In addition, a subscriber or authorized private hauler may not place a solid-waste or recycling container outside a building or structure without prior city permission approving its location.

**Exceptions / conditions:** Under subsection (5), the director may waive some or all requirements **except accessibility and location** if the customer demonstrates either (a) that the receptacle/container would not be visible at ground level from any adjacent right-of-way or property because code-compliant walls, fences, or other on-site screening already exists, or (b) enforcement would cause extreme hardship. Subsection (3) assigns the customer risk and indemnification obligations not material to enclosure construction.

**Source text:**
> “The enclosure will be constructed and maintained in such a manner as to ensure that the waste receptacle is not visible from ground level.”
>
> “The enclosure shall have a gate that is of sufficient width so that the waste receptacle is easily accessible by a front-end loading refuse collection truck. Said truck requires 12 feet of unobstructed access.”
>
> “The base of the enclosure shall contain a six-inch-thick reinforced concrete pad of a size suitable for the waste receptacle in use at that location.”
>
> “Those customers whose site receives containerized service and whose use of or improvement to the service site requires site plan approval or approval of minor change of use, shall also comply with the rules and regulations regarding enclosure dimensions, materials, location, and other physical attributes, as may be promulgated and amended from time to time by the director of solid waste/general services.”
>
> “No final building inspection shall be undertaken nor certificate of occupancy issued for any site which has not complied with the enclosure requirements contained in this section.”
>
> “The enclosures shall be constructed so that the recycling containers are not visible from any public right-of-way.”
>
> “All enclosures are required to be constructed and maintained in accordance with the City of Clearwater specifications pursuant to section 3-201.D of the Clearwater Community Development Code and City Engineering Index 701.”

**Groundrule interpretation notes:** This is a special-use enclosure regime, not a general fence rule. Preserve separately the visibility performance standard, the 12-foot access clearance, the six-inch pad specification, the nonwaivable accessibility/location rules, and the administrative approvals. The excerpt's “paragraph 5 subsection (b)” wording is unclear, and the two-column extraction may have displaced subsection (4)(a), so applicability cannot safely be finalized from this excerpt alone.

**Confidence:** High for the quoted requirements; Medium for exact subsection structure and applicability.

**Needs additional source review:** Yes — **NEEDS FULL SECTION REVIEW** of all of § 32.284, § 32.321, Community Development Code § 3-201.D, City Engineering Index 701, the director's current enclosure rules/specifications, and applicable building/safety-code provisions.

### [§ 32.336 — Gas meters and LP tank locations must remain accessible]

**Applicability:** Customer premises served by the Clearwater Gas System, specifically the point of service for gas meters/service regulators and propane LP tanks.

**Requirement:** The gas-system representative designates the meter or tank location, typically within ten feet of the premises corner nearest the gas main, in a location expected to remain accessible and “not expected to be enclosed by fencing or hedges.” Each meter, regulator, and LP tank must remain readily accessible for reading, inspection, repair, testing, replacement, and operation of the shutoff valve. The customer must keep vegetation and sprinklers clear.

**Exceptions / conditions:** “Typically” makes the ten-foot description nonabsolute; the gas-system representative designates the actual location. After notice of a deficiency, remedial action is mandatory and may include relocation of gas facilities to protect life safety and required clearances. The excerpt states no particular fence offset.

**Source text:**
> “The location of the meter or tank will be designated by the applicable gas system representative and will typically be within ten feet of the nearest corner of the premises to the gas main and in a location that is expected to be maintained by the customer as accessible, i.e., not expected to be enclosed by fencing or hedges.”
>
> “Each gas meter and service regulator and propane LP tank shall be installed in a location readily accessible for reading, inspection, repairs, testing and changing of the meter/tank and operation of the gas shutoff valve.”

**Groundrule interpretation notes:** A proposed fence cannot be treated as permissible merely because it satisfies zoning geometry if it would enclose or obstruct these facilities. This is an access/performance constraint, not an express numerical fence setback or universal ban on all nearby fencing.

**Confidence:** High

**Needs additional source review:** No for the extracted access rule; Yes before deriving any numerical clearance from external gas-system standards.

### [§ 11.04(1) — Cemetery-space enclosures]

**Applicability:** Cemetery spaces in sections 14, 15, and 16 of the city cemetery. This concerns burial-space memorial enclosures, not ordinary parcel fencing.

**Requirement:** Aboveground enclosures (as well as aboveground monuments and memorials) for any space in those cemetery sections are prohibited.

**Exceptions / conditions:** None stated for enclosures. Section 11.04 also says cemetery management/use rules are recommended by parks and recreation and approved by the city manager, must be kept on file, and may not conflict with the enumerated restrictions.

**Source text:**
> “Aboveground monuments, memorials, and enclosures for any space in sections 14, 15 and 16 are prohibited.”

**Groundrule interpretation notes:** Retained as a location-specific barrier/enclosure prohibition, but classify outside ordinary private-property fence logic unless cemetery-space improvements enter product scope.

**Confidence:** High

**Needs additional source review:** Yes — obtain the current city-manager-approved cemetery rules if cemetery property is in scope.

## Irrelevant / False-Positive Keyword Hits

The following keyword hits do not materially govern construction of a fence, wall, screen, gate, hedge, or similar property barrier and should not enter the first-pass rule inventory:

- Solid-waste definitions: wallboard, siding, and scrap shingles identify waste streams, not allowed fence materials.
- Appendix A, classification 038.170, “Fence erection (See note D) — 69.80,” is a fee/business-classification entry.
