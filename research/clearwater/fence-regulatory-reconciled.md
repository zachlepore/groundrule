## Scope, method, and source limitation

This reconciliation is limited to the two ordinance-context extracts present in the repository:

- `research/clearwater/fence-followup-context.txt` (the requested second-pass source); and
- `research/clearwater/fence-context.txt` (the underlying first-pass fence-keyword context).

The requested `research/clearwater/fence-regulatory-inventory.md` is **not present in the working tree or repository history as of this review**. Therefore, a literal rule-by-rule comparison to that inventory could not be completed. References below to “first pass” mean the fence-keyword context file, not the missing inventory. This missing input is itself a material reconciliation gap and must be resolved before this document is treated as a final audit.

The extracts reproduce hits with original PDF/text line numbers. Citations such as “follow-up context 35558–35611” refer to those embedded source line numbers, not this Markdown file's line numbers. The source appears to be the Clearwater Code of Ordinances, Supplement No. 90 in many locations, with older supplement pages interleaved. It expressly directs readers to a separate companion **Clearwater Community Development Code** (first-pass context 38676–38704). The extracts do not contain the substantive fence chapter of that companion volume.

### Classification key

- **NEW RULE** — substantive, relevant requirement found in the follow-up review and not established by the available first-pass context.
- **CLARIFIES EXISTING RULE** — adds scope, exception, measurement, or administration to a first-pass item.
- **POSSIBLE CONFLICT** — apparently inconsistent requirements; none can be confirmed from these extracts.
- **CROSS-REFERENCE / DEPENDENCY** — directs the analysis to another provision, standard, or body of law.
- **FALSE POSITIVE / NOT APPLICABLE** — keyword hit does not regulate fences, walls, barriers, screening, or their placement.
- **NEEDS FULL SECTION REVIEW** — potentially relevant, but the extract is incomplete or the controlling text is absent.

No rule is described as citywide unless the excerpt supplies enough scope to support that characterization. “Full-section review” means review of the complete, current section—including definitions, amendments, tables, figures, and incorporated standards—not merely a larger keyword window.

## 7. Screening / Enclosure Rules

## 13. Conflicts or Ambiguities

No true conflict between substantive fence rules can be demonstrated because the general fence provisions are absent. The following ambiguities remain:

1. **Missing requested inventory.** The absence of `fence-regulatory-inventory.md` prevents confirmation that every earlier conclusion has been preserved, corrected, or superseded.
2. **Mixed source vintages.** The extracts show multiple supplement numbers and historical editor's notes. A provision's appearance in the extraction does not by itself establish that it is current on the research date.
3. **Two-column interleaving.** § 32.284 is not sequential in the text extraction; subsection (5) is absent while fragments from it appear in the opposite column. Its exception/relief scheme cannot be reconstructed conservatively.
4. **“Adjacent” in § 28.06.** No distance or measurement method is given.
5. **Fence versus structure.** § 28.08 does not expressly mention a fence in the supplied text. Treating every fence as a covered “structure” would require a definition or authoritative interpretation.
6. **Silt-fence parent citation.** The operative subsection is visible, but the section number is not on the same extracted page. The table of contents suggests § 28.95; this should be verified, not asserted as final.
7. **Unidentified trimming article.** The missing left/right-page context makes it impossible to know the controlling height/width standard or whether fences are included.
8. **Business-tax amount.** The $69.80 fence-erection line is not a permit fee, and the extract does not establish its present validity.
9. **Ratproofing index.** The broad index heading may reflect indexing convention rather than a generally applicable enclosure rule.

Accordingly, there are **no items classified POSSIBLE CONFLICT** at this stage; there is insufficient overlapping substantive text to identify one.
